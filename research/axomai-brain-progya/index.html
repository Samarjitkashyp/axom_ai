1. The Historic Milestone for Assam

Until today, building intelligent conversational applications for Assam required relying on global models like OpenAI ChatGPT (GPT-4o), Google Gemini, or Anthropic Claude. However, these models suffer from what computational linguists call "The Tokenization Tax". Because their tokenizers were designed for English, a single Assamese word (such as ব্ৰহ্মপুত্ৰ or ঐতিহাসিক) is fragmented into 5 to 7 byte-tokens. This makes generation 5x slower, 5x more computationally expensive, and severely compromises reasoning ability.

Furthermore, global models lack genuine grounding in Assam's history. When asked about Ahom kings, Srimanta Sankardeva, or local agricultural cycles, they hallucinate facts or borrow vocabulary from Bengali. Axom AI Brain — Progya (প্ৰজ্ঞা) solves this permanently. It is Assam's sovereign 2.00 Billion parameter dense large language foundation model, possessing 68,096 native Assamese vocabulary tokens and pre-trained on 6,193,137 verified Assamese cultural, historical, administrative, and literary records.

3. Transformer Architecture & How It Works

Progya executes high-precision cognitive reasoning through four foundational pillars:

  1. Byte-Pair Encoding Tokenization: Input text is tokenized using Axom AI's custom 68,096 BPE vocabulary. Common words (মীনশূকৰ, কাৰ্বি আংলং, চৰকাৰী) are processed as single tokens rather than 5 disjointed bytes.
  2. Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE): Positions are encoded dynamically across an 8,192 context window, preserving long-range thematic coherence across multi-page Assamese legal and administrative documents.
  3. Dense Transformer Core with SwiGLU: 2.00 Billion active parameters configured with SwiGLU activation functions and RMSNorm pre-normalization, delivering superior mathematical stability and linguistic fluency.
  4. Repetition & Tone Calibration: Generating authentic Assamese narrative with top-k (40), top-p (0.85), and repetition penalty (1.2) prevents the mechanical loops seen in uncalibrated open-source models.

7. Quick Start: Python Integration

import torch
from transformers import AutoTokenizer, AutoModelForCausalLM

# 1. Load weights from private Axom AI repository
REPO_ID = "axom-ai/axomai-brain-progya"
token = "your_hf_access_token"

tokenizer = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained(REPO_ID, token=token)
model = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained(
    REPO_ID,
    token=token,
    torch_dtype=torch.bfloat16,
    device_map="cpu",
    low_cpu_mem_usage=True
)

# 2. Native Assamese reasoning
prompt = "এদিন অসমৰ এখন সেউজীয়া গাঁৱত এজন কৃষকে"
inputs = tokenizer(prompt, return_tensors="pt")

with torch.no_grad():
    outputs = model.generate(
        **inputs,
        max_new_tokens=40,
        do_sample=True,
        temperature=0.65,
        top_k=40,
        top_p=0.85,
        repetition_penalty=1.2,
        pad_token_id=tokenizer.eos_token_id
    )

response = tokenizer.decode(outputs[0][inputs["input_ids"].shape[1]:], skip_special_tokens=True)
print(f"Generated Assamese Response: {response}")

Why is tokenization so critical for Assamese AI?

Global models break Assamese words into 4 to 7 byte-tokens, which inflates compute costs, causes slow generation speeds, and breaks grammatical understanding. Axom AI Progya uses a native 68,096 BPE vocabulary that represents complete Assamese words in 1 to 2 tokens, delivering 3x higher efficiency.

Can Axom AI Progya run on a standard laptop without a GPU?

Yes. In bfloat16 mode, Progya occupies only 1.62 GB of physical RAM, making it fully operable on standard 8 GB RAM laptops and local edge hardware.

Progya was pre-trained and calibrated on 6,193,137 verified records covering Ahom Buranjis, Srimanta Sankardeva's Vaishnavite literature, state government notices, agriculture, legal documents, and cultural proverbs.
